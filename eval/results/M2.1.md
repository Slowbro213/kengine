## What changed since Checkpoint C

| Commit | Change |
|---|---|
| 464c70e | `quick` checks a claim as soon as an answer rests on it. Before, a claim that wasn't load-bearing when it was added was never checked. That was 7 of 22 blocked `quick` items in Checkpoint C. |
| 5ec277c | Formal names may start with a digit (`1984`), take whole numbers and drop apostrophes. These were the most common reasons a given was rejected. |
| 108905e | A step that names an undefined number is left to the arithmetic checker instead of failing the logic check. |
| b6be5c9 | A given whose formal form looks wrong is translated again: when it was rejected, or when it says everything (has no "if" part). |
| 7000d67 | Prompt: True needs the conclusion derived; not being able to refute it means Uncertain. |
| 414581b | An answer that rests only on givens is blocked. A retranslation that still says everything is refused. |
| 95ab5f7 | **The answer check** (below). |

**The answer check.** In the run before it, 4 of the 9 answers that passed were wrong. The gate checked that each step the answer rested on was sound, but not that those steps proved the answer. The model could cite verified but irrelevant claims and answer "True". Now the folio conclusion is translated along with the premises, and the final gate checks the label against it:

- **True:** the claims the answer rests on, plus everything under them, must imply the conclusion.
- **False:** they must imply its negation.
- **Uncertain:** the givens and verified claims must leave it open, and every given must have a formal form. Otherwise a missing given could be what settles it.

The check is skipped when the conclusion has no formal form, and under the `permissive` gate. When it applies, an answer may rest on givens directly.

### The model almost never says Uncertain

The model's answer (passed or blocked) against the gold label, over both M2.1 runs:

| Model said \ gold | True | False | Uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| True | 17 | 2 | **22** |
| False | 2 | 6 | 2 |
| Uncertain | 0 | 0 | 5 |
| not a label, or none | 1 | 0 | 3 |

32 of the 60 runs have gold Uncertain. The model said Uncertain in 5 of them, and said True in 22. It treats "nothing rules this out" as "this follows". This is the main reason its answers are wrong, and Checkpoint C showed the same pattern (18 of its 27 wrong or missing answers). The prompt change in 7000d67 didn't move it.

The checker catches this now. A True answer must show the conclusion, and it can't when the premises leave it open. But catching a wrong True doesn't turn it into a right Uncertain: the model, told its answer isn't shown, rewrites the same chain.

Compared with Checkpoint C, the steps that were never checked (7 items) are gone. Steps with no formal form fell from 10 items to 2. Translating the givens got slightly better: 161 of 170 have a formal form in `quick`, against 157.

**The answer check alone** was the last thing blocking 3 items:

- **1271, quick:** a wrong True (gold Uncertain), caught. In Checkpoint C `standard` and in the run before the check, this exact answer passed.
- **894, quick:** a right True, blocked. The answer rested on both branches of the case split but not on b1, the given that says one branch holds. So the check is right that the answer doesn't show the conclusion. The model never fixed it, because the feedback says "rest the answer on a claim that states the conclusion", not which given is missing.
- **289, standard:** a right True, blocked by a bad translation. The conclusion "Roderick Strong leads a professional wrestling stable" became `leads(roderick_strong, x) -> professional_wrestling_stable(x)`: everything he leads is a stable. b1 split "professional wrestling stable" into `stable` and `professional_wrestling`, so no claim can match it.

All the other blocked items also had unchecked or failed steps, so the answer check wasn't the only thing stopping them.

**A "doesn't follow" can be right where the model is wrong.** In 1208 (gold Uncertain) the model reasons that James has lunch either in the company or at home. The checker says that doesn't follow, which is right: the premise is about employees, and nothing says James is one. That gap is why the answer is Uncertain. The model never noticed it and answered True three times.

## Insights

- **The checker now makes the answers it passes trustworthy.** 10 of 10 passed answers were right on folio and 16 of 16 on gsm8k. Before this round, a sound-looking but irrelevant chain could get a wrong True through. It can't now.
- **The bottleneck is the model, not the checker.** Counting blocked answers, the model is right 13–15 times in 30, no better than the baseline's 16. Its main error is answering True when the premises leave the question open: 22 times out of 32.
- **Translation is no longer behind half the blocks.** It's still behind some, such as 289's conclusion and the 2–5 items per run with a cited premise that has no formal form. Most blocks are now steps the checker correctly rejects.
- **Next.** Because the model's own reasoning is the bottleneck, this is the point the owner asked to look at a logic fine-tune: here, teaching the model that an unproven conclusion is Uncertain. Two cheaper things could come first:
  - Feedback that names the missing premise, which would have fixed 894.
  - Letting the checker answer Uncertain itself when the givens and verified steps leave the conclusion open and every given is formalized. The check already proves that. It would turn some of the 22 wrong Trues into right answers without the model changing its mind.

## Follow-up: the two cheap fixes, and a rerun

Run on 2026-11-05 from `main` at 74dbd11, with the same setup. The runs are in `eval/results/m2.1-fixes/`.

### What was tried

- **Letting the checker answer Uncertain itself.** Dropped before it was built. Across both M2.1 folio runs, z3 left the conclusion open, with every given translated, in 27 sessions. Only 16 of them have gold Uncertain. In the other 11, a weak translation leaves open what the premises settle. Answering Uncertain there would let wrong answers through.
- **Feedback that names what's missing** (74dbd11). When the trusted claims would show the answer but what it rests on doesn't, the feedback names the claims to add.
- **A trust bug** (same commit). A verified step only shows that it follows from what it cites. It was trusted even when something under it was unchecked. In 549 that made the conclusion look proven. Now a step is trusted only when everything under it is.

**Fix** (badf07d): a given that still says everything after its retry is left without a formal form, so nothing can rest on it.

### The scenario check, tried and reverted

This tried to catch the 11 weak translations. When z3 leaves the conclusion open, it showed the model z3's two cases (the conclusion false, and true) and asked which premises, read as plain English, each case makes false. A flag would block Uncertain.

On the 27 open sessions it flagged all 27, including the 16 with gold Uncertain. Its reasons were mostly confused ("wait, let's re-evaluate"). It can't tell a weak translation from a right Uncertain, so it was reverted (72336f8, reverted in 8ba4abd).
